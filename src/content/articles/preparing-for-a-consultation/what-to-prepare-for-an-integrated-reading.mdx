An integrated reading looks at two things at once: the birth charts of the people in a home and the home itself. Each half has its own inputs, and the quality of the session depends on having them ready. This article lists what Astrologer Kavita asks clients to gather before an integrated consultation, explains why each item is needed, and describes how to obtain it — including the parts that people most often find difficult, such as an exact birth time and the compass orientation of a flat.

## What birth details do you need for the astrology part?

<Answer>
  For the astrology part of an integrated reading, Astrologer Kavita needs the date, time and place
  of birth of each person the reading covers, with the source of the birth time and how confident
  you are in it. Those three facts are enough to cast the kundli; nothing else about the chart needs
  to be prepared.
</Answer>

The <Term slug="kundli">kundli</Term>, the Vedic birth chart, is cast from three facts. The date is rarely in doubt. The place should be the town or city of birth, not the family's home town if that is different, because the chart is calculated for the latitude and longitude of the birthplace. The time is the one that takes effort: it fixes the <Term slug="lagna">lagna</Term>, the rising sign, and everything that follows from it, so it should be recorded to the minute where a record exists.

Look for the time on a hospital record or birth certificate, in a horoscope prepared soon after birth, or in the memory of someone who was there, and note which of these it came from. If the sources disagree, bring all of them. If there is no time at all, say so; the chart can still be read for tendencies, and the vastu part of the reading is unaffected. What matters is not perfection but an honest account of what you have, so that the reading can say which parts are firm.

If the reading is for a family home, gather the same three facts for each person who lives there, with their consent. A home is arranged around everyone in it, and the recommendation about who sleeps where depends on each chart, not only the client's.

## What do you need from your home for the vastu part?

<Answer>
  For the vastu part of an integrated reading, Astrologer Kavita needs a floor plan of the home with
  north marked, the compass direction of the main door taken from inside facing out, a photograph of
  each room from its doorway, and a note of who uses each room and for what.
</Answer>

The table sets out each item, why it is asked for, and the simplest way to obtain it.

| Item                     | Why it is needed                                                                            | How to obtain it                                                                           |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Floor plan               | The vastu grid is overlaid on the outline to locate each room in its direction              | Builder's plan, sales brochure, society records, or a proportionate hand sketch            |
| North marked on the plan | Without orientation the plan cannot be read at all                                          | Compass reading at the main door, transferred to the plan                                  |
| Facing of the main door  | Fixes the entrance zone and the eight directions                                            | Stand inside the door facing out; read a phone or hand compass held level, away from metal |
| Photographs of each room | Show use, light, clutter, furniture and the state of the entrance and centre                | One photograph per room from its doorway; one of the view from the main window             |
| Who uses each room       | The reading matches rooms to occupants' charts                                              | A short list: room, person, use (sleep, work, study, prayer, store)                        |
| Sleeping direction       | The direction of the head during sleep is part of the reading                               | Note which wall the head of each bed is against                                            |
| Fixed features           | Kitchen, bathrooms, columns, ducts and stairs cannot be moved and are read as they are      | Mark them on the plan if the plan does not already show them                               |
| Plot or building context | For a house, the plot's shape and slope; for a flat, the building's entrance and your floor | A site plan, a description, or a photograph of the building from the road                  |

The plan need not be professional. A sketch drawn on squared paper, with the rooms roughly to scale and the doors and windows marked, is enough, provided north is on it. The orientation is the item people most often get wrong, so it deserves its own section.

## How do you find the compass direction of your home?

<Answer>
  Stand just inside your main door, facing out through it, and take a compass reading with a phone
  or hand compass held level and away from steel and electrical panels; repeat it twice. The
  direction you face is the facing of the home, which Astrologer Kavita then uses to orient the
  plan.
</Answer>

Brochures, neighbours and estate agents are all unreliable on orientation. A brochure may be drawn with the road at the bottom of the page. A neighbour may call a flat "east-facing" because its balcony catches the morning sun, when the door faces north. The only reliable source is a compass reading taken at the door.

Hold the phone level, well away from the metal door frame, the electrical distribution board and any large appliance, and let the compass settle. Read the direction you face while standing inside and looking out. Take the reading again a metre further into the room, and once more on a different day if you can; if the readings agree within a few degrees, use them. Then draw an arrow for north on the plan, or simply write the facing of the door on it. If your phone's compass gives a bearing in degrees, send the number as well as the direction: a door at 350 degrees and a door at 10 degrees are both "north-facing" but sit differently on the grid.

For a house on its own plot, add the direction the plot slopes, if it does, and the position of the gate, the overhead tank, the borewell or sump and any outbuilding. For a flat, note which side of the building your flat is on and where the lifts and stairwell sit relative to it.

## What questions should you prepare?

<Answer>
  Prepare the two or three questions that matter most to you, in your own words, and any dates
  connected to them — a planned move, a marriage, a business decision. Astrologer Kavita reads the
  charts and the home in the light of those questions, so the session spends its time where you need
  it.
</Answer>

An integrated reading covers a great deal of ground, and without direction it can spend that ground evenly, which is rarely what a client wants. Two or three questions, written down, keep the session focused. They can be broad — "why has this home never felt settled" — or specific — "which of these two flats should we take", "is this a reasonable year to start the business", "why is my daughter not sleeping well since we moved". Dates help: the month you plan to move, the year a marriage is being considered, the period during which a decision must be made. A brief timeline of the last few years' major events also helps, because it lets the astrologer check the chart's dasha periods against what actually happened before reading forward.

If a question concerns another person — a spouse, a child, a business partner — bring their birth details with their consent, and say whether they know the reading is taking place. Questions about health, legal matters or money are welcome as context, but the reading will not substitute for a doctor, a lawyer or a financial adviser, and it will say so.

## How does an online session work across time zones?

<Answer>
  Online sessions with Astrologer Kavita are held by video or phone at a time that falls in working
  hours for both you and the practitioner, with the floor plan, photographs and birth details sent
  in advance so the session itself is spent on the reading. A written summary follows by email.
</Answer>

Most integrated readings are done online, and distance changes nothing about the content. The birth details are sent ahead, so the charts are cast before the session. The floor plan, the compass reading and the photographs are sent ahead too, so the vastu grid is already overlaid and the fixed features are already noted when the call begins. The session is then a conversation: the client's questions, the reading of the charts, the walk through the plan, and the recommendations.

Time zones are handled by choosing a slot that sits inside a reasonable window for both parties; the geo pages of this site show the overlap for each city the practice serves. Have the plan and photographs to hand during the call, ideally on a second screen or printed, so that you can follow the walk through the rooms. Where a live video walk-through of the home is useful, it can be arranged for part of the session, though the photographs usually suffice. A written summary arrives afterwards so that the ranked recommendations are not lost.

## What happens after the session?

<Answer>
  After an integrated reading, Astrologer Kavita sends a written summary listing the findings from
  the charts and the home and the recommendations in priority order — what to change first, what to
  change if convenient, and what to accept — so that a household can act over weeks and refer back
  to it.
</Answer>

The summary is the practical output of the session. It records the main points from the charts, the state of the home as read on the grid, and the recommendations ranked into three tiers. The first tier is usually two or three changes of room use, sleeping direction or workspace that the charts and the home together mark as most significant. The second tier is what would help if it can be done without difficulty. The third tier names the fixed features that cannot be changed, says how significant they are, and describes what the tradition offers as mitigation and how far that goes.

Families often act on the first tier within a week or two and the second over a few months. A follow-up session, shorter than the first, is available for reviewing what has changed, for a new question, or for reading a new home when a move happens; it uses the same charts and needs only the new plan and photographs.

## What do you not need to prepare?

<Answer>
  You do not need a prepared horoscope, a previous astrologer's report, gemstones or remedies bought
  in advance, a professional survey of your home, or any special knowledge of astrology or vastu.
  Astrologer Kavita casts the charts fresh, reads the plan you send, and explains every term as it
  comes up.
</Answer>

Clients sometimes arrive with a thick file: printouts from horoscope websites, a report from a previous consultant, a list of remedies someone recommended, an architect's drawing commissioned for the purpose. None of it is necessary. The charts are cast fresh from the birth details, because the accuracy of a chart depends on the calculation settings and it is better to control them than to inherit them. Earlier reports can be discussed if you want a second view on them, but the reading does not start from them. Remedies bought before a reading are, at best, premature. A professional survey is pleasant to have and not required; a sketch and photographs serve.

Nor do you need to know the vocabulary. Terms such as <Term slug="dasha">dasha</Term>, <Term slug="nakshatra">nakshatra</Term> or <Term slug="brahmasthan">brahmasthan</Term> are explained as they arise, and the glossary on this site defines each one for reading afterwards. What you bring is your birth details, your home, and your questions; the reading does the rest.

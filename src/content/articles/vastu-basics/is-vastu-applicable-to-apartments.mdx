<Term slug="vastu-purusha-mandala">Vastu shastra</Term>, the traditional Indian science of building,
was set down for a world of plots, courtyards and houses built from the ground up. A person choosing
where to place the kitchen could also choose where to place the well, the gate and the boundary
wall. Most people asking about vastu today live in an apartment: a slice of a larger building, on a
floor they did not choose, with a kitchen and bathrooms fixed by the developer's plan. It is fair to
ask whether the principles still apply. The short answer is that they do, but with different
emphasis, and the honest version of that answer needs to say clearly what an apartment dweller can
and cannot change.

## Does vastu shastra apply to a flat you do not own the land under?

<Answer>
  Vastu shastra applies to apartments because its principles concern the directions, light, air and
  use of the space you actually live in, not ownership of the land. Astrologer Kavita reads a flat
  as a dwelling in its own right, with the building's orientation as context, rather than treating
  it as an exception to the rules.
</Answer>

The foundation of vastu is the <Term slug="vastu-purusha-mandala">vastu purusha mandala</Term>: a square grid aligned to the compass, on which each zone of a dwelling is given a direction, an element and a character. That grid was drawn for plots, but nothing in its logic depends on touching the earth. A flat has a north-east corner and a south-west corner just as a bungalow does. The morning sun still enters from the east, the prevailing wind still comes from a particular side, and the occupants still sleep, cook, work and rest in particular places. Those are the things the mandala reads.

What changes is the scope of the reading. In a house on its own plot, vastu considers the plot's shape and slope, the position of the gate, the placement of the water source, the height of the building in each corner, and the rooms inside. In an apartment, the plot-level questions have been answered by the developer for the whole building, and the consultant's attention moves inward: to the flat's own outline, its entrance, its rooms and how they are used. The principles are the same; the unit of analysis is smaller.

Some practitioners argue that only the building as a whole has vastu, and that an individual flat is too small a fragment to read. The more common view, and the one that fits the tradition's own logic, is that every bounded dwelling has its own mandala. A flat is bounded by its walls and entered through its own door, and that is enough.

## What changes when the building is a high-rise rather than a plot?

<Answer>
  In a high-rise, the plot, the entrance to the building, the position of lifts, water tanks and the
  structure are fixed for everyone, so vastu shifts from what can be built to how the flat is used.
  Astrologer Kavita separates the elements you inherit from the elements you control before giving
  any advice.
</Answer>

The table below sets out which vastu considerations belong to the building and which belong to the flat. The distinction matters because advice about the first group is only useful before you buy, while advice about the second is useful at any time.

| Consideration                         | House on its own plot                   | Apartment in a high-rise                                             |
| ------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Plot shape, slope and boundaries      | Chosen or corrected by the owner        | Fixed for the whole building; read as context                        |
| Main entrance direction               | Chosen by the owner                     | Building entrance is fixed; the flat's own door is what is read      |
| Position of the kitchen and bathrooms | Planned by the owner                    | Fixed by the plan; can be worked around, rarely moved                |
| Heaviness of each corner (structure)  | Designed in                             | Fixed by the structure; adjusted with furniture and storage          |
| Open centre (brahmasthan)             | A courtyard or hall                     | The centre of the flat, kept clear by how it is used                 |
| Water source and drainage             | Well, tank and sump placed by the owner | Overhead tanks and drains shared; internal water use is what remains |
| Light and ventilation                 | Windows and courtyards designed in      | Fixed by the facade; curtains, mirrors and room use are the levers   |
| Room use and sleeping direction       | Fully in the owner's hands              | Fully in the occupant's hands                                        |

Two features of tall buildings deserve a note. First, height changes the environment: a flat on the twentieth floor gets more wind, more light and less shade than the same plan at ground level, and the classical advice about a cool north-east and a warm south-east has to be read against what the flat actually experiences. Second, the building's core — lifts, stairwells, service shafts — is a heavy, dense element in a position the occupant cannot influence, and a consultant notes where it sits relative to the flat rather than pretending it is not there.

## Which parts of a flat can you still influence?

<Answer>
  Within an apartment you control the use of every room, the placement of beds, desks and heavy
  furniture, what occupies the centre, the treatment of the entrance, light and colour, and what is
  stored where. Astrologer Kavita's apartment advice works almost entirely through these choices,
  which need neither permission nor building work.
</Answer>

Room use is the largest lever. Vastu attaches characters to directions — the south-west, <Term slug="nairutya">nairutya</Term>, is the heavy, stable corner favoured for the main bedroom; the north-west, <Term slug="vayavya">vayavya</Term>, is the zone of movement, suited to guests; the north-east, <Term slug="ishaan">ishaan</Term>, is kept open and light — and a flat with three bedrooms usually allows the occupants to decide which of them sleeps where. That single decision often does more than any object or colour.

Furniture placement is the second. The direction the head points during sleep, the wall a desk faces, where the heaviest wardrobe or safe stands, and whether the centre of the flat is a passage or a storage dump are all matters of arrangement. The tradition's preference for weight in the south-west and lightness in the north-east can be honoured with a wardrobe and a clear floor.

The entrance is the third. You may not choose which direction your door faces, but you choose whether the threshold is clean, lit and unobstructed, what a visitor sees first, and whether shoes and clutter gather there. The <Term slug="brahmasthan">brahmasthan</Term>, the centre, is the fourth: keeping it clear, light and in use as a living area rather than a store is available to almost every household.

Light, air and colour come last, not because they are unimportant but because they are the easiest to overstate. Curtains that let morning light in from the east, a window that is actually opened, a lighter palette in a dark corner — these are sensible and consistent with vastu, and they should be recommended plainly rather than dressed up as remedies.

## How do you find the orientation of an apartment?

<Answer>
  Stand inside the flat's main door facing out; the compass direction you face is the facing of the
  flat. Astrologer Kavita asks clients for that reading, a floor plan with north marked, and a few
  photographs — a phone compass held away from metal is accurate enough for a reading.
</Answer>

Orientation is the one piece of information a vastu reading cannot do without, and in an apartment it is easy to get wrong. The building's brochure may show north, but brochures are sometimes drawn with the road at the bottom of the page rather than north at the top. The building's entrance may face one direction and your flat's door another. Neighbours will tell you the flat is "east-facing" because the balcony gets morning sun.

The reliable method is to stand just inside your own front door, facing out through it, and take a compass reading. A phone compass is adequate if it is held level and away from the steel of the door frame and any electrical panel; take the reading two or three times and in two places to be sure. Then mark that direction on a floor plan. With north on the plan, the consultant can overlay the mandala, locate the eight directional zones, and see which rooms fall where. Photographs of each room from its doorway, and a photograph of the view from the main window, complete the picture and allow the reading to be done at a distance.

## What about a rented flat, or one you are about to buy?

<Answer>
  A rented flat is read for use and arrangement, since structure is off limits; a flat you are about
  to buy is read for its fixed features, since the choice is still open. Astrologer Kavita adjusts
  the reading to which of the two situations the client is in, rather than giving one generic list.
</Answer>

In a rented flat, everything in the "flat" column of the table above except structure is still yours to decide. A tenant can choose which room to sleep in, keep the centre clear, arrange furniture, treat the entrance well and open the right windows. What a tenant cannot do is relocate a kitchen or a toilet, and a reading for a tenant should not dwell on those; it should say what the fixed features are, whether any of them is significant, and move on to what can be done.

Before a purchase, the emphasis reverses. This is the moment to look at the things that will never change: the outline of the flat and whether it is cut or extended in a corner, where the kitchen and the toilets sit, whether the centre is occupied by a column or a duct, which direction the door faces and what lies beyond the windows. A consultant can compare two or three shortlisted units on these points, and a difference that is invisible in the sales brochure may be obvious on the mandala. This is also the stage at which the occupants' birth charts are most useful, because a home can be chosen to suit them rather than adapted afterwards.

## What are the common apartment vastu problems that are easy to correct?

<Answer>
  The most frequent, correctable apartment issues are a cluttered or dark entrance, storage or a
  toilet-adjacent bed in the north-east, the main bedroom in the wrong corner, a blocked centre, and
  a bed or desk placed without regard to direction. Astrologer Kavita finds that most of these are
  resolved by rearrangement, not renovation.
</Answer>

A few patterns recur in flats across every city. The entrance hall becomes the place where shoes, bags, bicycles and parcels accumulate, and the first impression of the home is of obstruction. The north-east room, which the tradition would keep open and light, is used as a store because it is small. The largest bedroom, placed by the developer wherever the plan allowed, is taken by the parents regardless of its direction, while a south-west bedroom better suited to them goes to a child or a guest. The centre of the flat is filled with a dining table pushed to one side and a heap of things that have no other home. Beds are placed against whichever wall the socket is on.

None of these needs a builder. Each is a matter of deciding, once, how the flat will be used, and each is the kind of change a household can make in a weekend. The more serious fixed issues — a kitchen in the north-east, a toilet in the centre, a flat missing its south-west corner entirely — are rarer, and when they exist the honest advice is to say so, describe what mitigations the tradition offers, and be clear about their limits. A companion article covers what can be corrected without demolition.

## How does Astrologer Kavita read an apartment?

<Answer>
  Astrologer Kavita reads an apartment from its floor plan, compass orientation and photographs,
  overlays the vastu grid to locate each room, and then reads the result alongside the occupants'
  birth charts, so that the advice reflects both the flat's fixed features and the people who live
  in it.
</Answer>

The reading begins with the plan and the compass reading, from which the eight directions and the centre are located on the flat's actual outline. Each room is then considered in its zone, and the fixed features — kitchen, bathrooms, structure, entrance — are noted as they are. The second pass is about use: who sleeps where, where the family eats and works, what the centre holds, how the entrance is kept. The third pass brings in the occupants' <Term slug="kundli">kundlis</Term>, the Vedic birth charts, because the same south-east bedroom means something different for a person whose chart already runs hot than for one whose chart needs warmth. Vastu and astrology are read as one method with two instruments, and the recommendations that follow are ranked: what to change first, what to change if convenient, and what to accept.

That last category matters. An apartment is a compromise by nature, and a reading that pretends every feature can be perfected is not being straight with you. The aim is a flat that supports the people in it as well as its fixed shape allows — which, in most cases, is considerably better than it does before anyone looks.

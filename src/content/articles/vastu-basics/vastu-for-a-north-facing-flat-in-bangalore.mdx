Bangalore (officially Bengaluru) is, for most people who arrive to work in it, a city of apartments. The
technology corridors that grew from the 1990s onward, Whitefield and ITPL to the east,
Electronic City to the south, the Outer Ring Road belt through Marathahalli, Bellandur and
Sarjapur Road, and Hebbal and Yelahanka to the north, are lined with gated communities and
high-rise towers. Buyers in these projects rarely get to choose their unit's orientation; they
choose a tower, a floor and a price, and the orientation comes with it. A question that comes up
often from the city is a variant of "our flat is north-facing, what
does that mean for vastu, and what should we do about the rooms?"

This article answers that question for the Bangalore apartment specifically: what north-facing
means, how the tradition rates it, how the city's climate and housing change the reading, and
what a room-by-room assessment of a typical north-facing unit looks like.

## What does "north-facing" actually mean for a flat?

<Answer>
  A north-facing flat is one whose main entrance is on the north wall, so a person stepping out of
  the front door faces north. It does not refer to the balcony, the road, or where most windows
  look. Astrologer Kavita determines a Bangalore apartment's facing from a compass reading at the
  main door, checked against the builder's plan.
</Answer>

Vastu shastra, the traditional Indian discipline of orienting and laying out buildings, was
written for plots and houses, and for a house the facing is unambiguous: the side with the main
gate and door. Apartments introduce confusion because the front door often opens onto an
internal corridor with no view, while the balcony, the pleasant side with the light, faces
somewhere else entirely. Estate agents and builders in Bangalore frequently describe a unit by
its balcony view ("east-facing, lake view") when the vastu facing is the door.

For vastu purposes the door wins. The entrance is where the household enters and leaves; it is
the mouth of the dwelling in the tradition, and its position on the plan is what practitioners
assess first. A flat with a north door and a south balcony is north-facing. A flat with a west
door and a north balcony is west-facing, whatever the brochure says.

Two more points of method. First, measure with a real compass at the door, standing a metre or
two inside the flat, away from the steel door frame and the electrical distribution board,
which are usually right there and will deflect the needle. Bangalore's magnetic declination is
small, so a careful compass reading is close to true north; no large correction is needed.
Second, check the builder's floor plan for the north arrow and see whether it agrees. In
several Bangalore projects the site plan is rotated for the brochure and the arrow is easy to
misread. When the two disagree, trust the compass.

Practitioners also divide each wall into segments, called padas, and rate an entrance by which
segment it occupies, not just which wall. On a north wall the segments closer to the
north-east half are generally preferred; a door hard against the north-west corner is rated
less well. Textbooks differ on the names and exact boundaries of the padas, so a consultant
should show you the division on your own plan rather than quote a rule.

## Why does the vastu tradition rate a north entrance well?

<Answer>
  The north is the direction of Kubera, vastu's guardian of wealth, and is associated with the water
  element, openness and growth. The north-east adjoining it is the most honoured zone of the plan. A
  north entrance brings people in through the lighter, more open half of the home, which is why
  Astrologer Kavita, like most schools, rates it favourably.
</Answer>

The scheme behind this is the <Term slug="vastu-purusha-mandala">Vastu Purusha Mandala</Term>, the grid of deities that the tradition lays
over any plot. The heavy, earthy deities sit in the south and south-west; the light, watery and
airy ones in the north and north-east. A home whose activity and openness are concentrated in
the north and east, and whose weight and privacy are in the south and west, is considered to
sit correctly on the mandala. A north door supports that arrangement, because the living room
and the movement of the family naturally cluster near the entrance.

It is worth being precise about what the tradition does and does not claim. Older texts rate
directions of entry for particular purposes and particular classes of building; the modern,
widely repeated ranking that places north and east entrances above south and west ones is a
simplification, and reputable practitioners in both North and South India will tell you that
a well-placed south door on a good plot beats a badly placed north door. The direction is a
starting advantage, not a verdict. Nor does any careful practitioner promise that a north door
produces wealth; the association with Kubera describes the quality of the zone, not an
assured outcome for whoever lives behind it.

For a Bangalore apartment the practical consequence is that a north-facing unit is a good
starting point and the work is in what lies behind the door.

## How do Bangalore's climate and housing change the vastu reading?

<Answer>
  Bangalore sits on the Deccan Plateau at roughly 900 metres, which gives it a mild, breezy climate
  with rain from both the south-west and north-east monsoons. In a north-facing Bangalore flat, the
  north side is bright but never harsh, the south-west bedrooms are warm without being punishing,
  and Astrologer Kavita finds the classical layout fits the climate unusually well.
</Answer>

Vastu was never climate-blind. Its preference for open, light north and east sides and closed,
heavy south and west sides is, among other things, a response to the Indian sun, which is
strongest from the south and the west in the afternoon. In cities with fierce summers, Delhi or
Ahmedabad, a west-facing wall of glass is a real hardship and the vastu advice to keep the west
heavy and closed is also thermal common sense.

Bangalore is different. At about 13 degrees north latitude and roughly 900 metres above sea
level, it has warm days, cool evenings and a long, moderate monsoon season with rain arriving
from the south-west between June and September and from the north-east in October and
November. Winds blow mainly from the west and south-west through the monsoon months and swing to the east
and north-east in the cooler season. A
north-facing flat here is comfortable on its north side all year and rarely gloomy, and its
south and west sides are warm rather than oppressive. Several of the city's older planned layouts,
Malleshwaram and Jayanagar among them, were laid out on grids that run close to the cardinal
directions, which is one reason so many older Bangalore houses face plainly north,
south, east or west. The newer towers along the IT corridors are frequently set at an angle to
the road to fit the site, so a flat sold as "north-facing" may in fact face north-north-west,
which is worth confirming.

The housing stock changes the reading in three ways.

First, apartments share walls, ceilings and floors. The neighbour's kitchen or toilet may sit
directly above your prayer corner. The tradition has no ancient ruling on this; sensible
practitioners treat what is inside your own walls as primary and the building's stack as a
secondary consideration. Second, most Bangalore apartments have a single balcony, usually off
the living room, and a utility area off the kitchen. In a north-facing unit the balcony is often
on the south, which means the outdoor, open space of the flat is in the zone the tradition
wants heavy. That is a genuine departure and one of the most common things to address. Third,
the overhead water tank, the sump, the lift core and the fire escape are fixed by the builder
for the whole tower. They are not yours to change, and a reading should say so rather than
treat them as personal faults.

## What does a room-by-room vastu reading of a north-facing Bangalore flat look like?

<Answer>
  A room-by-room vastu reading of a north-facing Bangalore flat maps each room to its direction and
  compares it with the traditional preference: living room in the north or north-east, kitchen in
  the south-east, master bedroom in the south-west, toilets in the west or north-west. Then
  Astrologer Kavita ranks the departures by how much they matter to the residents' horoscopes.
</Answer>

A typical two- or three-bedroom north-facing unit in a Bangalore tower places the front door on
the north, a living-dining space running back from it, the kitchen to one side near the front,
and bedrooms towards the rear (the south). That is a good skeleton. The table below shows what
the tradition prefers, what a north-facing plan usually offers, and what to do when they differ.
It is a reference, not a prescription for any particular flat.

Three of these deserve comment for Bangalore in particular.

The kitchen is the item most often flagged in north-facing Bangalore flats, because builders
tend to place it near the entrance for plumbing efficiency, which in a north-facing unit means
the north-east or east. A kitchen in the north-east is a real departure in the tradition. In
an existing flat the remedy is internal: cook on the south-east side of the kitchen, put the
water purifier and sink on the north or north-east wall, keep the kitchen scrupulously clean
and bright, and give the rest of the north-east quadrant of the flat the openness the kitchen
has taken from it. Nothing about this needs demolition.

The south balcony is the second. Bangalore's climate makes a south balcony pleasant, but the
tradition wants the south-west corner solid. Filling a south or south-west balcony with heavy
planters, a stone bench or solid built-in seating, and keeping it as a used room rather than an
empty ledge, is the usual approach.

The work-from-home study is the third, and it is a Bangalore specialty. Many families in
Whitefield and Electronic City have converted the third bedroom into an office. Vastu prefers
the worker to face north or east and the desk to be in the north, east or north-east of the
room, with a solid wall behind the chair. In a north-facing flat the natural home for the study
is the front half of the unit, near the living room, which is where the light is anyway.

## How does a horoscope change the advice for a north-facing flat?

<Answer>
  A horoscope changes which of a north-facing flat's departures matter most. Each direction is
  linked to a planet, and a planet that is weak or running its major period in a resident's kundli
  marks the zone to correct first. Astrologer Kavita reads the Bangalore floor plan and the family's
  birth charts together, so the advice fits the people in it.
</Answer>

Two families can move into identical north-facing flats in the same tower in Sarjapur and
receive different priorities. If the main earner's <Term slug="kundli">kundli</Term> (the Vedic birth chart) shows a strong
Mercury, the planet associated with the north, the north door and north living room are working
with the chart and need little more than being kept clear. If the chart shows a weak Venus and
the family is in a Venus <Term slug="mahadasha">mahadasha</Term> (the major planetary period of the Vimshottari dasha system),
the south-east, Venus's direction in the most common scheme, is where the effort goes: that is
the kitchen question above, and it becomes the first item on the list rather than the fourth.

This is the point of an integrated reading. Vastu alone gives a list of departures with no way
to rank them except by textbook severity. Astrology alone describes the timing and tendencies in
a person's life with no way to act on the physical environment. Together, the chart tells you
which zone to attend to and when, and the plan tells you how.

If you are looking at a north-facing flat in Bangalore, or already live in one, the practical
starting kit is simple: the builder's plan with a north arrow, a compass reading taken at the
main door, a photograph of each room from its doorway, and the birth date, time and place of the
people who will live there. From those, a full reading can be done remotely, and the
corrections that follow are nearly always ones you can make yourselves.
